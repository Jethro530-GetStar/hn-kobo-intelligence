An ongoing cyberattack has reportedly struck major supermarket chains across Europe, disrupting refrigeration control systems. The attack — which has reportedly seen units set to continuously defrost at 50 degrees Celsius — is also apparently affecting deliveries and in-store pricing powered by electronic shelf labels, La Dépêche reports.
According to the report, "several" major retailers have been targeted by a cyberattack, which has hit Danfoss-made refrigeration controls used by French supermarkets Intermarché, Leclerc, and Coopérative U. Indeed, the latter's president, Dominique Schelcher, has confirmed as of Monday morning that "A cyberattack has been affecting the refrigeration production facilities of several stores in our network since last night." Schelcher said teams had been mobilized to fix the issue and promised an investigation into the incident, which he described as "extremely serious."
The report states the units have had their automatic defrosting systems activate, but that hackers have also configured the function — which normally runs for just two hours a day — into a continuous, forced defrosting cycle with the temperature set to a toasty 50 degrees Celsius. Sources reportedly described "a wave of heat" escaping from fridges and freezers
Early reports indicate losses are already approaching 300,000 euros, but could run into several million euros nationwide. Separately, NOS in the Netherlands reports that several supermarket chains have been hit with a malfunction due to a cybersecurity incident on Sunday afternoon. Albert Heijn, Etos, and Gall & Gall have had their websites taken down, impacting deliveries. Electronic shelf labels have also been compromised, disrupting in-store pricing. "We have immediately taken measures to protect our IT environment. Part of this involves the preventive temporary shutdown of a number of systems," Albert Heijn said in a statement.
It is unclear at this stage whether the two attacks are related or how widespread either of them is. Belgium's Delhaize chain, which belongs to Albert Heijn, has also reported disruption. Images online show stores in Amsterdam warning customers about price problems, and the suspension of loyalty programs that have also been impacted by the incident.

Article
An open-source parametric 3D CAD application built in pure Rust.
Sketch, constrain, and build solids with a parametric feature timeline. Change a dimension and watch your model rebuild. Run it on your desktop, in your browser, or let AI agents model through the built-in MCP server.
A Raspberry Pi 4 case from an empty design: a sketch dimensioned with the board's parameters, extrude, fillet, shell, the rim rounded in one pick, an M2.5 standoff patterned on the 58 × 49 mm hole rectangle, port cut-outs, vent slots, "Pi 4" engraved in DejaVu Sans Bold and a PETG material defined in the design, with its mass in Properties, then height 24 → 32 mm in Parameters and it all rebuilds. 9× playback. Full video, recorded with the demo script.
- Windows: download and run the installer. Also for ARM64 and 32-bit PCs, or as a portable zip that runs without installing.
- macOS: open the disk image and drag SolidCraft into Applications (Apple silicon and Intel).
- Linux: download the AppImage, allow it to run as a program and open it.
Or install the .deb (Ubuntu, Debian, Mint) or .rpm (Fedora,
openSUSE), or use Flatpak.
With Snap: sudo snap install solidcraft (Snap Store, or the .snap file).
For ARM64: AppImage, .deb, .rpm.
- Web: try it in your browser, no install.
The downloads aren't signed yet. On Windows, if SmartScreen warns you, choose More info, then Run anyway. On macOS, the first time, right-click SolidCraft in Applications and choose Open.
Older versions, checksums and other formats are on the releases page. Install details, upgrading from earlier builds and building from source are in Getting started.
Documentation: the SolidCraft book (user guide, developer guide and command reference; sources in docs/book).
Status: early but usable for simple parts. 75 of 76 public reference parts match their analytic or FreeCAD-computed volume, area, centre of mass and bounding box (docs/oracle.md); the other is a tracked bug. These checks do not establish complete geometric or workflow equivalence. The commands SolidCraft has are listed by area in docs/features.md; status and plans are in ROADMAP.md. Open work and known bugs are in GitHub issues, and help is welcome: see CONTRIBUTING.md.
SELENE, a scientific rover built entirely through SolidCraft's MCP server by @0xlillith: 3,323 editable timeline steps that rebuild with no errors, replayed here at 2× speed. Full video · download the editable model.
- Sketches on origin planes, offset planes or planar faces: lines, rectangles, circles, arcs, polygons, slots, ellipses, fit/control splines, conics and text; construction geometry, projection, trimming, fillets and chamfers, including corners at the origin. New face attachments retain supported construction identities and chosen occurrences; missing sources expose an explicit plane repair workflow. See face attachments. Explicitly closed fit splines and closed associative offsets use periodic C2 interpolation. Supported convex line-and-arc offsets retain their identity when an inset consumes corner arcs, and linked extrusions follow the current boundary. Breaking the association freezes the boundary and keeps existing extrusions usable. Curve drawing and picking adapt together to zoom and display scale, including small circles at Fit and sketches in placed components.
- Constraints and dimensions solved by our own solver. Points the constraints fix relative to known geometry are placed exactly by construction (loci intersections and rigid-cluster decomposition, after Owen 1991 and Fudos–Hoffmann 1997); Levenberg–Marquardt solves the rest, and the rank of its Jacobian gives the remaining degrees of freedom. Constraints: coincident, point on curve, horizontal/vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, curvature (G2), equal, concentric, collinear, midpoint, symmetry, fix; linear, aligned, horizontal, vertical, point-to-line, radius, diameter, angle, arc sweep, arc length and slot length dimensions driven by parameters. Over-constraining edits are rejected; fully constrained geometry turns black. Dragging solves the sketch every frame and rebuilds the model once, when the drag ends. Select a line or its two endpoints, type its dimension and press Enter to commit one undo step; with Sketch Dimension it's D, click, type, Enter. Fit keeps sketch geometry clear of the palette and navigation controls.
- Profiles found automatically, including regions split by lines and holes inside outlines. Newly captured complete adaptive boundaries retain their outer and hole source identities when spline span counts change. Partial or ambiguous boundaries do not qualify.
- Features on a parametric timeline: extrude, revolve, sweep, loft, fillet/chamfer, shell, draft, holes, threads, patterns, mirror, split, combine, move and construction geometry. The timeline rebuilds incrementally; features can be edited, suppressed, renamed, reordered and deleted. Supported geometry and current limitations are described in ROADMAP.md. New revolves preserve symmetric and two-sided angular placement across reversed axes; older designs retain their saved convention when reopened or edited.
- Parameters and configurations with unit-aware expressions (2 * width + 5 mm, angle / 2), ordered rules, feature suppression and per-row body materials/appearances.
- Assemblies with nested components and occurrences, joints, motion studies, contact sets, interference and material-aware mass properties. Recorded body transfers preserve modelling history and world placement; 3D sketch wires retain their coordinate frames through edits.
- Sheet metal with flanges, hems, fold/unfold/refold, material cutouts, flat patterns, retained shallow prismatic pockets, exact line/arc/circle DXF outlines and bend tables; plastic enclosure features and rules. Pocket depth and side survive unfold/refold and appear separately from through-hole outlines in the flat preview and DXF.
- Files: design containers (.solidcraft) with lossless binary mesh payloads;
plain JSON remains supported for opening, Save As and export. Desktop Open can show a saved
provisional picture from warm geometry caches while rebuilding before editing; caches never
replace the parametric design. Export to STEP (AP242 with body names, colours and
components as assemblies; revolved faces as native planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori),
IGES 5.3 (manifold solid B-reps with names and colours), 3MF, STL (binary/ASCII) and OBJ;
3MF, STL and OBJ import as mesh bodies (they render, measure, move and export; solid features
need B-rep bodies). Convert Mesh reconstructs supported closed constant-section parts
as parametric solids with planar and circular cylindrical faces;
STEP import (AP203/AP214/AP242 solids, assemblies, units, names, colours) and IGES import
(manifold solid B-reps, or trimmed and bounded surfaces sewn into solids) as a base feature
that later features build on — open a .step/.stp/.igs/.iges file, insert one into a
design, or drop it on the window (docs/step-import.md).
- Desktop app (egui + wgpu): toolbar with workspace tabs, browser, timeline, 3D viewport with axis view and navigation bar, sketch tools, feature dialogs, command palette, and a JSON-lines control channel so agents and tests can drive it. Feature errors and Help can open an editable Create Issue draft with relevant context and an optional reviewed screenshot; opening GitHub does not submit the report.
- Headless CLI solidcraft-cli: run command scripts, measure (volume, area, centre of mass,
face/edge/vertex counts), export, render PNG snapshots and replay reference recipes.
- MCP server solidcraft-cli mcp: AI agents model, measure and look at parts headless or
in the running app (see below).
cargo run --release -p solidcraft -- --sample # desktop app with a sample part
cargo run --release -p solidcraft-cli -- commands # the command registry
cargo run --release -p solidcraft-cli -- run examples/bracket.json --out bracket.step
cargo run --release -p solidcraft-cli -- snapshot examples/bracket.json --out bracket.png
cargo run --release -p solidcraft -- part.step # open a STEP file as a new design
cargo run --release -p solidcraft-cli -- eval part.step # measure an imported STEP file
cargo xtask ci # fmt, clippy, tests, assets, layers, wasm
Windows builds: cargo xwin build --release --target x86_64-pc-windows-msvc -p solidcraft.
On Windows and Linux, SolidCraft detects a connected 3Dconnexion SpaceNavigator or SpaceMouse through its multi-axis HID interface. Move the cap to pan and zoom, and tilt or twist it to rotate the 3D view. While editing a sketch, the cap pans and zooms without changing the sketch view. The Navigation page in Preferences has an on/off switch and a speed control for the 3D mouse.
On Linux SolidCraft reads /dev/hidrawN directly, so the logged-in user needs access to the
device. A udev rule that adds the uaccess tag does that, for example
/etc/udev/rules.d/70-3d-mouse.rules for devices with 3Dconnexion's vendor id:
SUBSYSTEM=="hidraw", ATTRS{idVendor}=="256f", TAG+="uaccess"
Older SpaceNavigators use Logitech's vendor id 046d; add a rule with their product id (see lsusb)
instead of matching every Logitech device. Reload with sudo udevadm control --reload && sudo udevadm trigger, then replug the device. The Flatpak
sandbox does not expose hidraw devices, so the 3D mouse only works with native builds there for now.
Devices that send all six axes in HID report 1 (some homemade ones do) are recognised from their
report descriptor, so they work as well.
In the browser: cd apps/solidcraft-web && trunk build --release --public-url ./ (WebGPU, or
WebGL2 with ?webgl; ?sample opens the sample part).
A script is a JSON list of commands, the same ones the UI runs:
{"commands": [
{"command": "sketch.create", "params": {"plane": "XY"}},
{"command": "sketch.rectangle.two_point", "params": {"p0": [0, 0], "p1": [40, 30]}},
{"command": "solid.extrude", "params": {"distance": 20}},
{"command": "solid.fillet", "params": {"edges": [[0, 0, 10]], "radius": 3}}

solidcraft-cli mcp is a Model Context Protocol server on
stdio. Headless it runs its own design session; with --connect 127.0.0.1:PORT it drives the app
started with solidcraft --control PORT, so you can watch the agent model. Tools:
list_commands, execute, batch, inspect_design, measure, body_topology,
set_parameter, screenshot (returns the image), export, undo, redo, new_design, open,
save. Claude Code: claude mcp add solidcraft -- solidcraft-cli mcp. Details, client
configuration and an example session: docs/mcp.md.

9. Ask HN: Do you want a router that has DD-WRT pre-installed?

Article
I sometimes run across comments where someone says they want an open-source router. I wonder if this is a viable idea for someone to start as a small company. What makes me hesitant is that, first, it looks like this already exists in several wireless and non-wireless routers (like the GL.iNet Flint or OpenWrt One), and second, I assume hackers would just make their own.
I have been flashing DD-WRT to my routers for many years. My current setup is an Archer C9 that connects via cable to an Archer C8, which then wirelessly bridges to an R7000. All of them run DD-WRT. If I had "fun money," I would buy a low-power SFF PC with dual NICs, run OPNsense or OpenWrt, and use the wireless routers strictly as access points. Since I don't have that much to spend, I look for older DD-WRT friendly routers, usually under $30, on Craigslist or Facebook Marketplace, and flash them myself.
I see that Ubiquiti and MikroTik make quality routers with proprietary OSes for around $65 new. Protectli and Netgate offer pfSense routers starting at $259.00. The GL.iNet Flint 2 is $169.00, and the OpenWrt One is $89.
According to ResellBot (because eBay now requires a login to see sold listings with prices), the sold "DD-WRT Routers" generally go for under $160. These include the WRT3200ACM, Netgear AC1750, Linksys EA9300, and Linksys R7350.
When I asked an AI how I would go about judging interest, building, raising funds, and manufacturing, it seems doable but expensive (said $125,000 minimum in total made up of $30,000 for regulatory approval, engineering and protoyping $10,000+) and time-consuming just to get to a "golden prototype" for demos.
So if I were a "hacker" and wanted an open-source router, depending on my funds, I would either:
1. Flash DD-WRT to an existing router (used or new).
2. Buy one with it already installed, like the OpenWrt One or a Protectli device.
3. Get an SFF PC and build my own.
I remember when some comments about DropBox said it was a waste because any hacker could make that. I also know DropBox was marketed to the average person and businesses. I don't really see how a hacker friendly router could take off like that unless hackers are choosing the hardware and setting up the networks for average people. And I assume that most people setting up the networks want different qualities like brand name recognition, ease and speed of purchasing equipment (Walmart or BestBuy), and the ability to point a customer or family member to the number of solid reviews and articles saying it a good router to qualify their choices.
So, do you actually want a router that you can buy with DD-WRT pre-installed?

Article
Gateway is an open-source server that lets a business deal with AI agents (e.g. Meta's Muse, Grok Bot, Dots) directly. Agents find it through llms.txt, then look up services and prices, book, cancel or reschedule, and leave the owner a message that they answer from Slack. It is modular: turn on only the connectors you need. Missing one? Open an issue.
Needs Node >= 22.13, git and npm (macOS or Linux; Windows via WSL), plus the system you want agents to book into: a Google Calendar shared with a service account, or a Square Appointments account. The wizard walks you through setup for either (Google, Square).
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mudroom-ai/gateway/main/install.sh | sh
The installer clones into ~/gateway, builds, and runs the setup wizard, which starts by offering to read your website to prefill its questions (optional, and it asks before sending the page text to a model), then asks what you want agents to do. It writes config.json and .env, checks access, and prints the next steps: start the server with npm start and check it locally. Re-run it to update; npm run init re-opens the wizard, which asks whether to keep, review or start over. Details, manual install and the read-it-first variant: docs/install.md.
The API (services, slots, quotes, booking, cancel, reschedule, owner messages) is described in docs/agents.md and served at /openapi.json.
To repeat this recording with your own agent, give the local server a public HTTPS address (cloud agents can't reach localhost) and hand the agent the link. Needs cloudflared (brew install cloudflared).
# Terminal 1: open a tunnel. It prints https://<tunnel-host>.trycloudflare.com
cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:3000
# Terminal 2: start Gateway and let it answer on that hostname
cd ~/gateway && ALLOWED_HOSTS=<tunnel-host>.trycloudflare.com,localhost,127.0.0.1 npm start
Then ask your agent (Muse, Grok or similar), filling in one of your services:
Book me a <service> on Friday at 10am using
https://<tunnel-host>.trycloudflare.com
My name is <name> and my phone number is <phone>.
The agent reads the instructions served there (the same page as /llms.txt), checks slots, gets a quote and books, and the event shows up in your calendar. The tunnel hostname changes on every run, and ChatGPT refuses trycloudflare.com links, so use a real domain for anything longer-lived (docs/deploy.md §3).
Connectors come in three modules. A Store is a booking platform that owns its slots and prices. A Calendar is a plain calendar that Gateway builds slots and prices on. Communication is how the owner hears from Gateway, and it works beside either of the others.
| System | Module | Doc |
|---|---|---|
| Google Calendar (service account) | Calendar: Gateway builds slots and prices from your rules | docs/connectors/google.md |
| Square Appointments | Store: Square's catalog, prices and policies; buyer-level access, no payments | docs/connectors/square.md |
| GlossGenius (experimental) | Store, shadow mode: services, prices and open times read from the public booking page; bookings go to a shared Google Calendar plus a text to the merchant, who adds them to GlossGenius | docs/connectors/glossgenius.md |
| Twilio SMS (optional) | Communication, notices only: texts the merchant about every agent booking, cancel and reschedule | docs/connectors/twilio.md |
| Slack (optional) | Communication, two-way: agent messages land in a Slack channel and the owner answers by typing in the thread; booking notices too. Socket Mode, no inbound URL | docs/connectors/slack.md |
Adding a connector: docs/connectors/README.md. Contributing: CONTRIBUTING.md.
