1. FAA issues national ground stop – lifted 4.5 hours later

Article
Thousands of flights were canceled or delayed Saturday morning as airlines struggled to recover from a nationwide ground stop that halted U.S. departures for more than four hours Friday.
By 9:30 a.m. ET Saturday, flight tracking service FlightAware had logged more than 3,700 cancellations and 1,990 delays. Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, Chicago O’Hare International Airport and New York’s John F. Kennedy International Airport were among the hubs experiencing the most disruptions, according to FlightAware.
The Federal Aviation Administration lifted the ground stop at 8:15 p.m. ET Friday. More than 7,000 flights were canceled Friday, and airports across the country filled with stranded passengers. Delta Air Lines, United Airlines and American Airlines said Friday evening that they were resuming flights.
A senior government official told NBC News on Saturday that the system that caused the outage had been identified and taken offline. The FAA said a router sent too much data, overloading older air traffic control equipment.
What caused the ground stop
Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Friday evening that controllers began seeing inconsistencies in radar data and communications with aircraft at 3:36 p.m. ET. The FAA issued the ground stop at 3:54 p.m. ET, “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety.”
Duffy said the FAA retained some radar and communications capabilities and halted departures as a precaution. Medical flights, including those transporting organs for transplant, were allowed to continue.
Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Friday night that authorities had found no evidence of a cyberattack.
“FAA was able to handle it. We did it out of an abundance of caution,” he said in a news conference from the White House Friday night. Earlier on Friday, he described the issue as a telecommunications outage.
Why disruptions continued Saturday
Airlines had to get planes and crews back into position after the ground stop, and some crews may have reached limits on how long they could stay on duty. Duffy said he expected air traffic to recover before the Thanksgiving rush.
But by 6 p.m., Reagan Washington National Airport had run out of room to park aircraft and Chicago O’Hare International Airport was nearing capacity, a source with knowledge told NBC News.
Deveren Werne was on an American Airlines jet that was about to take off from Indianapolis to Chicago when suddenly the pilots announced that all flights were grounded.
“At first, the pilot announced that there was some kind of radio issue,” Werne, 55, of Greenville, South Carolina, told NBC News in a telephone interview. “Then a little while later, the pilot said something like, ‘There is something going on that’s more serious than that.’”
Werne said he began hearing from friends who were also stuck on planes, including one on a runway in Chicago. His plane headed back to the terminal so passengers could get off.
Friday’s ground stop is perhaps the most severe consequence of what Duffy described as a decades-old air traffic control system that uses analog equipment, which has led to several issues and outages over the past few years.
In May 2025, radar screens at Newark Liberty International Airport went black during a 90-second outage.
Duffy said the legacy equipment will continue to fail and pushed Congress to approve $50 billion to overhaul the system. Congress last year approved $12.5 billion over five years to upgrade air traffic control, Reuters reported.
Mullin said although authorities cannot rule out a cyberattack, there was no evidence to suggest there was one. “There’s nothing completely certain right now. It doesn’t appear that way,” he said.
Meanwhile, Air Space Intelligence, the AI tech startup that is a vendor for the FAA on its new SMART system for air traffic control, insisted its software was not responsible for the nationwide ground stop.
“The current ground stop is not based on or a result of the SMART system, any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC,” the company said in a statement posted to X.
Duffy said the SMART system is “completely disconnected” from the equipment that suffered issues Friday.
Several hours after the ground stop was announced, a top aviation expert estimated that anywhere from 300,000 to 400,000 passengers were stranded — and it was anybody’s guess when they would get to their destinations.
“Daily, we have about 25,000 flights in U.S. domestic markets,” Vikrant Vaze, a Dartmouth professor who specializes in commercial aviation logistics, wrote in an email to NBC News. “I would say we should have around 3,000 to 4,000 flights held up at this time over the past 2 plus hours.”
With planes stranded on runways, the likelihood increased that some of these planes would have to be diverted to other airports, the source said.
Aviation safety expert Jeff Guzzetti called the outage “extraordinary.”
Guzzetti emphasized that pilots are trained to operate through communications outages and that there are redundancies in place for such instances. Pilots “could get information relayed through other means” to ensure planes are spaced properly for landing.
There are different layers of communications — high-altitude, midlevel and the towers on the ground. The high-altitude control centers are the ones affected by the outage.
Nationwide ground stops are extremely rare
Ground stops across the nation are a rarity in U.S. history.
After the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks, the FAA ordered an unprecedented nationwide ground stop. All civilian air traffic across U.S. airspace was halted and airborne planes were ordered to land at the nearest available airports.
In January 2023, the FAA grounded all flights for a few hours, blaming a failure in the Notice to Air Missions system, which alerts pilots to a host of potential dangers.
Officials at the time blamed contract personnel for the outage, saying they accidentally deleted files while working to correct the synchronization between databases. The error prompted a computer outage that lasted approximately an hour and a half, Department of Transportation officials said at the time.

4. Why get an expensive Pi when I have an old phone?

I’m working on a project that needs a high resolution camera, GPS, storage, and a microcontroller to control some lights. (More on the project soon!) I’ve been designing PCB adapters around a Raspberry Pi, an Arducam 64MP camera, and a GPS module. Every time I add another part, the bill grows.
I’m also trying an ESP32-P4 board, which will get its own post soon. But the alternative I keep coming back to is an old smartphone.
I have a Redmi 13C 5G and a Huawei Mate 30 5G. Phones like these turn up second hand on Taobao for roughly ¥300–500 (£33–56 / €40–67 / $45–75). The Redmi gives me a camera that saves 50MP JPEGs; the Mate 30 has a 40MP main camera and a telephoto lens. Both already contain GPS, storage, a clock, a battery, mobile data, and a touchscreen. Their USB-C ports give me a route to the PCB that will handle the lights and other sensors.
Here is the rough parts comparison I made while deciding what to build next:
| What I need | Used Redmi or Mate 30 | Raspberry Pi 4 approach |
|---|---|---|
| Computer | Included in phone price | Pi 4, 4GB: ¥880 / £98 / €118 / $132 |
| High resolution camera | Redmi 50MP JPEG; Mate 30 40MP | Arducam 64MP: ~¥400 / £45 / €53 / $60 |
| GPS | Included | GPS HAT: ~¥153 / £17 / €20 / $23 |
| Storage | Built in | 64GB microSD: ~¥50 / £6 / €7 / $7 |
| Core total | ¥300–500 / £33–56 / €40–67 / $45–75 | ~¥1,483 / £165 / €198 / $222 |
| Clock | Included | RTC HAT: ~¥80 / £9 / €11 / $12 |
| Battery backup | Included | UPS HAT: ~¥133 / £15 / €18 / $20, plus cells |
| Touch display | Included | 5″ touchscreen: ~¥247 / £28 / €33 / $37 |
| Mobile data | Built in, with a SIM | 4G HAT: ~¥527 / £59 / €70 / $79 |
| More fully equipped total | ¥300–500 / £33–56 / €40–67 / $45–75 | ~¥2,317 / £258 / €310 / $347, plus batteries |
These are illustrative November 2026 listings. The more fully equipped Pi total uses the cellular HAT’s GPS instead of buying the separate GPS HAT; its modem is 4G, while the phones are 5G models. Neither side includes my custom light controller PCB, a SIM or data plan, or an enclosure. The conversions use 10 November 2026 mid market rates of approximately ¥1 = £0.111531 / €0.133643 / $0.14991, rounded to whole units in the table.
The phone is already becoming more than a price exercise. I’ve unlocked and rooted the Redmi, tested its camera, and got it sleeping and waking for scheduled work. I can plug it into my computer and have Codex configure and test the pieces I need. The Huawei is harder to root, but I can still run a kiosk app on it. I’ve made a stand to photograph the same target with the Redmi and Arducam side by side; I want to see what detail each camera actually captures. A 50MP JPEG file does not, by itself, settle that question.
The open question is supply. Finding two good used phones is easy; finding a few hundred matching ones, with healthy batteries and predictable camera behaviour, would take more work. I feel increasingly confident that it’s possible.
This could be useful well beyond the current project. For digital artworks and installations, we’ve often used a Pi or a Mac mini. Imagine instead posting a client a configured phone: plug it into the display and it runs. With mobile data and remote access, I can fix something without asking a gallery for Wi-Fi privileges or an open port.
There’s something fun about taking a device made in enormous numbers, giving it a second life, and finding that most of the hardware I was about to assemble is already inside it.

A 64-bit Out-of-Order Superscalar RISC-V Processor (RV64IMAFDV + Zba + Zbb + Zicsr + Zifencei) booting mainline Linux 6.6.50 and BusyBox userspace under Sv39 virtual memory.
Fursoy Slice-1 (fursoy_s1_core / fursoy_s1_soc) is a clean-slate 64-bit out-of-order superscalar RISC-V processor implemented in SystemVerilog, featuring Sv39 demand paging, machine/supervisor/user privilege separation, cycle-exact hardware performance telemetry, and 500 MHz Nangate 45nm standard-cell synthesis closure across 8 core functional blocks.
- 2-Wide Superscalar OoO Core (fursoy_s1_core):
- 2-wide fetch, decode, register rename, and in-order retirement (M/S/U privilege modes, medeleg/mideleg, WFI, MRET/SRET).
- 128-entry Reorder Buffer (ROB_DEPTH = 128) with 4-bit wrap-safe epoch tags and precise exception/interrupt flush recovery.
- 160-entry Physical Integer Register File (PRF_SIZE = 160, 128 speculative free-list entries) plus 8 branch checkpoints (NUM_CHECKPOINTS = 8) for single-cycle speculative RAT rollback.
- Dedicated issue queues: 32-entry Integer IQ (INT_IQ_DEPTH = 32), 32-entry Memory IQ (MEM_IQ_DEPTH = 32), FP IQ (FPRF_SIZE = 64), and decoupled 8-entry Vector IQ (VLEN = 256, ELEN = 64, 48 vector physical registers).
- Parallel-prefix 64-bit Kogge-Stone fursoy_s1_alu and fursoy_s1_branch_unit (single-cycle execution + carry-free target equality), 3-stage pipelined multiplier, and hardware divider.
- Sv39 Virtual Memory & Non-Blocking Caches:
- Dual-port Sv39 DTLB: 64-entry 4-way set-associative base TLB (16 sets, Tree-PLRU) + 8-entry 2 MiB / 1 GiB superpage CAM with 32-entry Translation Request Table (TRT_DEPTH = 32).
- Sv39 ITLB: 64-entry 4-way set-associative base TLB + 8-entry superpage CAM.
- Shared Hardware PTW (fursoy_s1_ptw): Walks Sv39 page tables with non-destructive L1D dirty snoop coherency so page-table writes sitting dirty in L1D are observed directly without eviction, paired with SFENCE.VMA store-queue drain and D-cache clean synchronization.
- L1I Cache (fursoy_s1_l1i): 16 KiB, 4-way set-associative (64 sets × 64 B), VIPT-safe with conservative next-line prefetcher and 2 I-MSHRs.
- L1D Cache (fursoy_s1_l1d): 16 KiB, 4-way set-associative (64 sets × 64 B), 8-bank (PA[5:3]) non-blocking data cache with 16-entry Load Queue, 16-entry Store Queue, 4 MSHRs, and 2-entry Writeback Buffer.
- SoC & Peripherals (fursoy_s1_soc):
- 5-to-1 Priority Crossbar: Core MMIO > PTW walks > L1I refills > L1D refills > L1D dirty writebacks, with anti-starvation fairness and unmapped-aperture bus error trapping.
- Platform Devices: 256 MiB DRAM interface (0x8000_0000), CLINT (0x0200_0000), PLIC (0x0C00_0000, 31 external sources), 16550 UART (0x1000_0000), and custom minimal SBI v2.0 M-mode firmware (Base, TIME, SRST, DBCN).

Unified input and output token limit
Gemini 4 models have a unified 1,048,576-token limit that includes both input and output tokens (where output includes thinking, function calls, and the final response). Gemini 4 models support LOW, MEDIUM (default on
gemini-4-carbon and gemini-4-argon), and
HIGH (default on gemini-4-hydrogen,
gemini-4-carbon-cyber-permissive, and
gemini-4-argon-cyber-permissive). gemini-4-hydrogen
also supports XHIGH for extended reasoning depth:
from google import genai
from google.genai import types
client = genai.Client()
response = client.models.generate_content(
model="gemini-4-carbon",
contents="Design a lock-free ring buffer in Rust for multi-producer, single-consumer telemetry ingestion.",
config=types.GenerateContentConfig(
thinking_config=types.ThinkingConfig(
thinking_level=types.ThinkingLevel.HIGH,
),
),
)
print(response.text)
Implicit context caching
Gemini 4 models support implicit caching for shared prompt prefixes of 4,096 tokens or more, with durable caching enabled by default. To benefit from it, put static content, such as a code repository or long documents, at the start of your prompt.
Agentic video understanding
Agentic video understanding
is generally available. It's off by default: uploaded videos use
media_processing="static". To turn it on, set media_processing to
"agentic".
Sampling parameters
Gemini 4 models ignore custom values for temperature, top_p,
and top_k, as Gemini 3 models do. To control output, use
thinking_level and structured output (response_schema).
Migrate from Gemini 3 models
When migrating from Gemini 3.8 Flash to Gemini 4 Hydrogen, or from Gemini 3.1 Pro to Gemini 4 Carbon or Gemini 4 Argon, account for the following behavioral differences in Gemini 4 models:
| Area | Gemini 3 models | Gemini 4 models | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input and output token limits | Separate input (1,048,576) and output (65,536) limits | Unified 1,048,576-token limit across input and output (where output includes thinking, function calls, and the final response); 65,536-token default single-request output limit (32,768 tokens for non-streaming direct HTTP requests on 4 Carbon and 4 Argon), extensible up to 1,048,576 tokens using long decodes | Budget input and output together. Upgrade to the latest Google Gen AI SDK and set max_output_tokens greater than 65,536 (or use background=true or continuation_token in custom loops) on the Interactions API or GenerateContent for outputs over the single-request limit. |
| thinking_budget | Mapped to a thinking level | Returns an HTTP 400 error | Use thinking_level. |
| thinking_level="MINIMAL" | Supported | Returns an HTTP 400 error | Use LOW. |
| Explicit context caching | Supported | Not supported | Remove cached_content references and put static content (4,096 tokens or more) at the start of your prompts. |
| Multi-turn and parallel thought_signature handling | Lenient duplicate signature handling | Only the first functionCall part in a parallel turn carries thought_signature (omitting it in the active turn or duplicating it onto sibling functionCall parts returns HTTP 400); stripping thought_signature values from earlier turns degrades reasoning quality and can cause MALFORMED_FUNCTION_CALL finish reasons; same-model prompt-prefix edits return HTTP 400 by default (prefix_mismatch_behavior="ERROR") | Preserve thought_signature across all previous turns (both functionCall and final text parts), keep it only on the first functionCall part in a parallel turn, and set prefix_mismatch_behavior="DROP" in ThinkingConfig if your harness edits or compacts earlier conversation turns. |
| Token billing | A subset of tokens is billed | Every token the model processes is billed, including thinking tokens and internal tool system instructions and function declarations when using grounding. Grounding with Google Search is still billed per query. | Review your cost estimates. |
To migrate from gemini-3.8-flash or
gemini-3.1-pro-preview, do the following:
- Change the model ID to gemini-4-hydrogen (from gemini-3.8-flash) or gemini-4-carbon or gemini-4-argon (from gemini-3.1-pro-preview) and upgrade to the latest
Google Gen AI SDK (google-genai >= 2.34.0 for Python, @google/genai >= 2.32.0 for JavaScript/TypeScript).
- Account for the unified 1,048,576-token input and output limit and the
65,536-token single-request output cap (32,768 tokens for non-streaming
foreground calls on 4 Carbon and
4 Argon), or configure
long decodes for
larger outputs on the Interactions API or GenerateContent.
- Replace thinking_budget and thinking_level="MINIMAL" with thinking_level set to LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, or XHIGH (XHIGH is
supported on gemini-4-hydrogen).
- Remove explicit cached_content references.
- Preserve thought_signature across all previous turns (both functionCall and final text parts), keep it only on the first functionCall part of
parallel tool-calling turns, and set prefix_mismatch_behavior="DROP" if
your application compacts or edits prior turns.
- Remove temperature, top_p, and top_k, which have no effect.

10. Show HN: Agweb – a browser for agents that stays out of your way

Article
A browser for coding agents on macOS, built on WebKit (Safari's engine). I run several coding agents at once, and Playwright's Chrome windows kept stealing my keyboard, while headless browsers got blocked by some sites and left me guessing what went wrong. agweb is one window that never takes focus: agents drive it from the shell with agweb, a playwright-cli-style command, and you watch them all live.
Idle tabs are suspended: on my Mac five open pages sit at 0% CPU, where Chrome's GPU process kept five to seven cores busy.
| Five idle pages | Memory |
|---|---|
| agweb | 590 MB |
| Headless Chrome | 730 MB |
| Playwright Chrome | 1.6 GB |
- Never steals focus. No window comes to the front, nothing pops up. Dialogs, changing tabs, file pickers and downloads are answered in code.
- You can always see it. The dashboard (⌘D) shows every tab of every agent at once, live, with each agent's last command. And it is always a real window: some sites block headless browsers, and headless leaves you guessing what went wrong.
- Many agents at once. Each agent works in its own workspace with its own tabs, grouped by agent in the sidebar.
- Log in once. A named profile (-p work) keeps its logins and is shared by every agent using it, at the same time.
- Light. Idle tabs are throttled and then suspended, so open pages cost almost nothing once nobody uses them.
- Acts like a person. Clicks and keys are native macOS events, and the pointer moves like a hand, so hover menus and drag-and-drop work.
- Made for agents. Snapshots give each element a ref that lasts while it is on the page. snapshot --diff and find show only what matters, and every action reports what it changed.
- Passwords stay out of the transcript. fill --secret-cmd 'pass show site' types a secret the agent never sees in replies. 1Password and Bitwarden CLIs work too.
- No ads. Ads and trackers are blocked with uBlock Origin's lists, which agweb downloads every 4 days. An agent testing analytics can turn it off for its own workspace (agweb -w task adblock off).
- Demo videos. video-start records a tab to MP4, with the pointer and clicks drawn in.
Needs macOS 26 or later. Download Agweb.dmg from Releases, drag Agweb to Applications and open it. Then Help ▸ Install Command Line Tool and Help ▸ Install Agent Skill (for Claude Code and Codex). Both are links into the app, so they update with it.
To build from source instead (Xcode 26 or later):

agweb -w task snapshot # the page as a tree with [ref=eN] handles
agweb -w task click 'role=button[name="Main menu"]'

+ - list [ref=e1167]

agweb help lists every command. Settings (⌘,) has text size, dashboard columns, a CPU and memory readout and the ad blocker switch.
Same commands where possible, so agents already know them; a Playwright name it lacks suggests its own (scroll-to → scroll-into-view). On top:
- Replies say what changed: fetches started, lines added or removed, dialogs, popups, downloads, console errors. No snapshot after every step, so far fewer tokens.
- Refs last while the element is on the page.
- ask hands the tab to you for a login, MFA code or CAPTCHA, without taking focus.
- Actions wait for the fetches they caused, so agents rarely sleep.
- text returns the page's article as markdown.
- Shared logins: agents on one profile run at once; a Chrome profile opens in one browser only.
- WebKit only: no Chrome DevTools Protocol, so Playwright and Chrome extensions can't attach, and Chrome-only sites may misbehave.
- route mocks fetch and XHR only; requests lists images, scripts and styles by URL only (--static).
- The ad blocker can't run uBlock's scripts, so YouTube ads and some "turn off your ad blocker" walls get through.
- For what it can't do, use Playwright.
