Canada launches the Real-Time Rail, bringing 24/7 payment infrastructure to the national economy
The new system will enable participating financial service providers to clear and settle payments in real time, providing a made-in-Canada platform for future innovation and prosperity.
OTTAWA, ON, November 9, 2026 – Payments Canada today announced the launch of Canada’s Real-Time Rail (RTR). This new national payment system will transform day-to-day payments for Canadian businesses and consumers by enabling instant speed and settlement, real-time cash-flow visibility and data-rich messaging through existing banking and payment channels.
The launch of Canada’s RTR marks a major milestone in modernizing the critical infrastructure that underpins Canada’s economy and future growth. The RTR will enable participating members, including financial institutions, credit unions and payment service providers, to exchange, clear and settle payments securely in real time for their customers, 24 hours a day, every day of the year.
“A stronger, more productive economy relies on modern financial infrastructure that can match our ambition,” said the Honourable François-Philippe Champagne, Minister of Finance and National Revenue. “The launch of Canada’s Real-Time Rail lays the foundation for a more dynamic and competitive digital economy, driving innovation, expanding choice and creating new opportunities for Canadians while positioning our country for long-term growth.”
“The launch of Canada’s Real-Time Rail marks the beginning of a new era for payments in our country,” said Susan E. Hawkins, President and CEO, Payments Canada. “As a national asset that’s designed specifically for our country, the RTR gives the payment ecosystem a modern foundation to build on. By collectively leveraging this rail, our ecosystem will unleash its full potential, enabling our members to build capabilities that drive innovation, resilience and growth for businesses and consumers across the nation.”
Ron Morrow, the Honourable François-Philippe Champagne and Susan E. Hawkins
The RTR will drive significant cost savings and economic growth in Canada over the next decade. The C.D. Howe Institute projects that, with its functionalities at launch, the RTR will generate up to $14.5 billion in cost savings from lower processing costs, reduced use of cash and cheques, and reduced float (the period during which funds are temporarily unavailable to the recipient). Extending beyond these cost savings, the RTR is expected to generate $16 billion in economic gains, growing to $27 billion as new functionalities are introduced over time.
“The Real-Time Rail is an important step in modernizing Canada's payment infrastructure and ensuring it keeps pace with Canadians' evolving needs,” said Tiff Macklem, Governor of the Bank of Canada. “The Bank will oversee the system so that it operates safely and reliably while ensuring Canadians can benefit from innovations in financial services.”
Owned and operated by Payments Canada, and built in collaboration with delivery partners CGI, Deloitte Canada, IBM Canada and Interac Corp., the RTR leverages ISO 20022 data-rich messaging. Through this shared technical language, the RTR holds the potential to strengthen Canada’s economic sovereign autonomy and support its global competitiveness.
Fraud safeguards from day one
Canada is the first G7 country to deploy a real-time payment system with mandatory centralized fraud services for use at launch. These services will be used alongside participants’ existing fraud controls to mitigate the risk of fraud for Canadian businesses and consumers at multiple levels. Fraud controls required at the launch of Canada’s RTR include:
- Confirmation of payee (available for consumer accounts on day one)
- A fraud transaction “safety” score
- A risk list or repository of fraud risks and indicators
- A fraud reporting and intelligence platform
While no system is entirely immune to risk, these measures establish a strong, coordinated layer of defence across the payment network.

The initial cohort of active RTR system participants includes:
- Wealthsimple

Canada’s deliberately sequenced RTR rollout pairs day-one security with rapid scalability. With additional participants onboarding continually in the coming months, including all major Canadian financial institutions and other members applying for access, the RTR will be available to more than nine in 10 Canadians within the first year after launch.
Made-in Canada, for Canada
The launch of the RTR reflects our ‘Team Canada’ approach, bringing together regulators, members, stakeholders and delivery partners around a shared vision. Built in Canada and designed to serve all Canadians, the RTR provides accessible payment infrastructure through existing banking and payment channels. This infrastructure supports a diverse country and a broad range of communities, including small businesses, newcomers and people in rural areas.
Payments Canada research found that 60 per cent of Canadian businesses are ready to adopt real-time payments to address everyday challenges such as forecasting cash flow and waiting to send and receive funds. More than half of surveyed consumers (52 per cent) also expressed interest and intent to use real-time payments.
To meet this evolving need, the RTR serves as a secure platform for innovation. With it, system participants can build and offer products and services that benefit consumers and businesses across the country, supporting a changing economy and Canada's future prosperity.
An infrastructure for the future
Canada’s Real-Time Rail is an important step toward a more productive, innovative and inclusive economy, and the work will continue to ensure the country’s payment infrastructure meets Canadians’ evolving needs.

About Payments Canada
Payments Canada enables prosperity, productivity and safety for Canada through trusted, critical national payment infrastructure. As a public-purpose organization, we own and operate: Lynx, our high-value payment system; the Automated Clearing Settlement System (ACSS), our retail batch payment system; and the Real-Time Rail (RTR), our new real-time payment system. We establish the by-laws, rules and standards that govern these systems, which cleared and settled $103 trillion in 2025 — more than $411 billion every business day. EDIT 2: I should've mentioned that MeowDisplay started as a fork of OpenDisplay: https://github.com/peetzweg/opendisplay
which provided the foundational screen capture, virtual display, encoding and iPhone/iPad receivers. I've since taken it in a more phone-first direction, with work on the interaction, streaming and security layers. I've credited OpenDisplay and its developer Philip in the README under "Built on OpenDisplay".
MeowDisplay is my take on the control your Mac from your phone scene. Available on iPhones, iPads and Macs, with Android rn in closed testing.
The website has interactive illustrative demos if you'd like to see the app demonstrated.
I think MeowDisplay does a few things differently, some of which I prefer and you might too.
Such as Window view: allowing multiple tabs in split-screen type panes. Traditional Mirror/Extend views exist as alternatives.
Floating windows: coming in the 2.1 release.
Touch input approaches: Direct Touch(Classic or Natural), experimental Smart Touch, and Trackpad mode alongside Apple Pencil support. Doesn't sacrifice display space for a trackpad, use all of the phone's screen with your preferred mode.
Stage Peek: shows a scrollable and searchable list of your Mac's windows on your phone along with an app drawer when you hold and pull on the left edge of your screen.
It's free and open source so you can try it and see if it's for you without having to commit to it or anything.
EDIT: I've used MeowDisplay during development whilst I was travelling between cities, left my acbook at home clamshell, powered, external display attached, and used it for small tasks remotely, it was pretty reliable even on a 5G network on a moving bus.

Theoretical physics
Claude Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in theoretical physics. Both concern black holes, and the effort to unite the two linchpin theories of modern physics, relativity, and quantum mechanics.
Both of Claude’s results rely on holographic duality, the idea that certain universes containing gravity can be precisely described by a quantum theory containing no gravity at all. In the first problem, physicists in 1983 had shown that as these universes heat up, black holes form abruptly, like water boiling; but their method worked only in large universes that were big enough for relativity to apply. A calculation to determine whether the same was true in very small universes, where only the quantum description can be used, was laid out in 2003 but was too laborious to complete. Fable 5.2, working largely autonomously, completed it and found the transition is abrupt there, too. In the second problem, Fable 5.2 found the exact limit of a technique physicists have used for more than 15 years to rule out candidate theories of quantum gravity in a simplified universe. Anti-distillation. Distillation, where a model is trained on the outputs of a more advanced one to extract the advanced model’s capabilities, occurs illicitly on an industrial scale, often involving thousands of fake Claude accounts. Fable 5.2 has the strongest protections against distillation of any model we’ve released; it uses the techniques we described in a recent report, which have reduced the data harvested by distillers by 90% to 99%.
We are also expanding the rollout of an API feature that helps guard against illicit distillation: preserved thinking now applies to all accounts. Preserved thinking prevents distillers from editing Claude’s prior context in an attempt to extract its reasoning (developers can refer to our preserved thinking docs to learn how to update integrations with new API features).

Claude Code reads files, writes programs and runs them, typically with the same access as the person using it. Goldman's own CIO was promoting traders' AI-built tools in July, though.
